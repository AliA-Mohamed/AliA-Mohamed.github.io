Research

Metabolism as a readout of disease

Most of the diseases I work on are defined by a faulty protein: a polyglutamine-expanded androgen receptor, a mutant glucocerebrosidase, an aggregating chaperone client. My question is what that protein does to the cell's energy and lipid metabolism, and whether those changes can be measured, modelled and eventually treated.

Theme 1
PhD · 2023 to now

Mitochondrial dysfunction in spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy

SBMA (Kennedy's disease) is caused by a CAG repeat expansion in the androgen receptor gene. It is usually framed as a motor neuron disease, but muscle is affected directly. I built six MyoD1-inducible iPSC lines from SBMA patients and controls, including an isogenic pair, and showed impaired myogenic differentiation, fragmented mitochondrial networks and a remodelled lipidome in patient myotubes.

  • Mitochondrial morphology by confocal and electron microscopy, with automated image analysis
  • Membrane potential, ROS and mitochondrial mass by flow cytometry
  • Untargeted metabolomics and lipidomics at Leiden University (COST PRESTO STSM, 2026)

Theme 2
MSc · 2022 to 2023

Microglia metabolic reprogramming in GBA1-linked Parkinson's disease

Mutations in GBA1 are the most common genetic risk factor for Parkinson's disease. My master's thesis proposed that they push microglia into a different metabolic state, changing how they talk to neurons. I tested this in cell models with transcriptomic profiling in Milan, then characterised microglial phenotypes in GBA mutant mice at DZNE Bonn.

Theme 3
2025 to now

Computational metabolomics and multi-omics modelling

At Biosfer Teslab, within the MSCA CardioSCOPE programme, I worked on AVANTE, a project building a cardiovascular risk tool for women from capillary blood. I analysed NMR lipoprotein metabolomics and Olink proteomics from 268 participants and trained machine-learning and deep-learning models to quantify metabolites directly from NMR spectra.

NMR metabolomicsOlink proteomicsPLS · XGBoost · CNNregularised models

Biosfer Teslab, Tarragona, Spain

Theme 4
2026 to now

AI for rare-disease research

The SBMA Research Agent is an autonomous literature system I built for the SBMA field. It ingests PubMed, CrossRef and Semantic Scholar, has curated about 1,000 SBMA papers from 1968 to today, and uses LLMs to extract more than 10,000 structured findings. It writes a living textbook, a monthly newsletter and a map of open research gaps.
